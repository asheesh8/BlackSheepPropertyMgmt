# Black Sheep Property Management — Site Content System

Working master for the rebrand of Jeffrey Danaher Property Management.

Source reviewed: [danaherpropertymanagement.com](https://danaherpropertymanagement.com) on August 7, 2026. The current site is used only for factual service and business information. Its voice, visual style, structure, and claims have not been carried forward automatically.

## 1. Brand and content direction

### Positioning

Black Sheep Property Management is a local property care and maintenance company for homes and businesses in Chittenden County, Vermont. The offer is practical and broad: grounds, interiors, winter work, and specialty upkeep from one team.

### One-sentence description

Black Sheep keeps Chittenden County homes and businesses clean, cared for, and ready for every Vermont season.

### Short descriptor

Grounds. Interiors. Winter. And the jobs in between.

### Voice

- Premium, but never precious.
- Local and plainspoken.
- Specific about the work.
- Confident without unsupported superlatives.
- Dry sheep humor appears as an occasional reward, not the whole personality.
- Short headlines; useful body copy; direct calls to action.

### Language to avoid

- “Best-in-class,” “world-class,” “premier,” and “unparalleled.”
- “Transform your property” unless a genuine transformation is being shown.
- “Hassle-free,” “stress-free,” and “focus on what matters most.”
- Fake metrics, rankings, guarantees, testimonials, or response-time promises.
- Language that implies leasing, tenant placement, rent collection, or financial property management unless Jeffrey confirms those services.
- A sheep pun in every section.

### Naming rules

- First reference: **Black Sheep Property Management**
- Later references: **Black Sheep**
- Owner: **Jeffrey Danaher**
- Never use “Black Sheep Landscaping” as the umbrella brand unless it becomes a separate service division.

**Visual action:** A white flock moves forward. One black sheep stops, looks toward the camera, turns against the flow, and moves away. The camera follows.

**Copy beats:**

1. As the flock advances:

   > Most follow the herd.

2. As the black sheep stops and turns:

   > We look closer.

3. As the camera follows:

- Primary: **Request an estimate**

## 15. Mobile, motion, and accessibility notes

### Mobile cinematic behavior

- Preserve the narrative order—herd, pause, turn, Black Sheep—but shorten the pinned sequence.
- Keep the black sheep inside a center-safe crop throughout the supplied 720p video.
- Show the business name and service descriptor without requiring the user to finish the animation.
- Never lock the user into a long scroll-controlled film.
- Keep the estimate and phone actions visible immediately after the brand reveal.
- If bandwidth is poor, show the poster frame and transition directly to the practical hero copy.

### Reduced motion

- Replace the animated sequence with a still frame of the black sheep facing against the herd.
- Display all essential hero copy at once.
- Disable parallax, camera-follow simulation, marquee movement, and auto-advancing galleries.
- Cross-fades should be brief or removed entirely.
- The static experience must retain the same content and calls to action.

### Video and content accessibility

- Treat the sheep film as decorative; it should not contain unique information.
- Use an empty alt treatment for decorative video/poster media and expose the hero text as real HTML.
- Muted autoplay only. No surprise audio.
- Include pause control if meaningful motion continues after the opening reveal.
- Maintain readable contrast over every video frame with a tested overlay, not text shadow alone.
- Do not put paragraph text over detailed photography on mobile.

### Mobile content hierarchy

1. Brand name and exact offer
2. Estimate and call actions
3. Four service divisions
4. Real work
5. Jeffrey’s local story
6. Contact details and hours

### Performance-aware copy behavior

- The first useful headline and actions must render before the background video is ready.
- Use a lightweight poster frame as the initial visual.
- Lazy-load gallery media below the first service overview.
- Avoid placing essential service names inside images or animation frames.

## 16. Client confirmation list

The following claims or decisions need Jeffrey’s confirmation before launch:

1. **Business name and legal status:** Is “Black Sheep Property Management” the registered business name, a DBA, or a public-facing brand?
2. **Meaning of property management:** Does the company provide maintenance/property care only, or also leasing, tenant, rent, inspection, or financial-management services? This draft intentionally promises only maintenance and care.
3. **Service area:** Is all of Chittenden County accurate? Are any nearby counties or specific towns included or excluded?
4. **Base location:** May the site publicly say Essex, Vermont? Is there a publishable street or mailing address?
5. **Phone:** Confirm (802) 343-7779 as the public estimate and service number.
6. **Email:** The current site’s email is not readable in the available page content. Provide the correct public address.
7. **Hours:** Confirm “Everyday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.” and whether those are office, estimate, or service hours.
8. **Free estimates:** The current site says “Get a Free Estimate.” Confirm that all listed services qualify before adding “free” to calls to action.
9. **Experience:** Confirm the statements that Jeffrey worked under his father for 15 years and has been in business for 18 years. Decide whether the 18 years should be updated for 2026.
10. **Residential and commercial scope:** Confirm which of the four divisions serve residential properties, commercial properties, or both.
11. **Grounds scope:** Confirm landscaping, grass cutting, mulching, seasonal cleanup, and walkway/outdoor upkeep. Add planting, edging, pruning, fertilization, or other services only if offered.
12. **Cleaning scope:** Confirm janitorial, commercial, residential, carpet, and upholstery services; typical frequency; and whether disinfecting/sanitizing claims are appropriate.
13. **Winter scope:** Confirm plowing versus other removal methods, parking lots/driveways/walkways, roof shoveling, deicing/salting, service contracts, and whether any emergency or 24-hour response exists.
14. **Pool scope:** Confirm cleaning and maintenance. The old site also mentions chemical balancing and inspections; do not publish those until confirmed.
15. **Pond scope:** Confirm cleaning, maintenance, and fish stocking. The old site also mentions algae control and aeration; do not publish those until confirmed.
16. **Handyman scope:** Define accepted work and exclusions, especially tasks requiring licensed electrical, plumbing, or structural contractors.
17. **Estimate workflow:** Confirm preferred contact method, expected response time, photo uploads, text-message consent, and who receives submissions.
18. **Project photography:** Confirm that all supplied photographs and images from the current site can be reused under the Black Sheep brand, and identify any clients/addresses that must remain private.
19. **Operating promises:** Approve the proposed About/Mission language before it is treated as a public company standard.
20. **Trust details:** Provide only verifiable insurance, licensing, certifications, associations, review profiles, and warranties. None are claimed in this draft.
